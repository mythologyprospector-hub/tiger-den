# Tiger Den Law

**Status:** Foundational operating law
**Scope:** Tiger Den only

These rules govern how Tiger Den research is conducted and how claims enter the map.

## 1. Reality Before Elegance

The corpus determines the map. Tiger Den's architecture, taxonomy, and hypotheses must yield to evidence.

## 2. Never Invent a Primitive

A proposed primitive is not a catalog fact merely because it is plausible, useful, or repeatedly described by an agent. It requires traceable evidence from existing material.

## 3. Similar Is Not Identical

Source similarity, naming similarity, shared dependencies, or common purpose do not by themselves establish primitive equivalence. Equivalence requires comparison of meaningful contracts and behavior.

## 4. Preserve Distinctions That Matter

Implementations must remain distinct when differences in contract, behavior, invariants, security properties, performance characteristics, portability, resource requirements, licensing, or operating assumptions are materially relevant.

## 5. Unknown Is First-Class

When evidence is insufficient, record the uncertainty rather than filling the gap with inference presented as fact.

## 6. Provenance Is Mandatory for Strong Claims

A claim about an implementation must identify enough source information to permit the claim to be checked again. When version or commit identity matters, record it explicitly.

## 7. Separate Observation From Interpretation

Observed facts, derived measurements, interpretations, hypotheses, and decisions must not be blended into a single undifferentiated assertion.

## 8. Prefer Existing Evidence Over New Code

The project's first responsibility is discovery and mapping. New code is justified when it improves surveying, normalization, analysis, verification, storage, or presentation of the map—not merely because writing code is possible.

## 9. Do Not Destroy the Terrain

Tiger Den must not modify, replace, or claim ownership of source material merely to catalog it. External implementations remain under their own repositories, licenses, and governance.

## 10. Respect Licensing and Provenance

Cataloging a location does not grant rights to copy, redistribute, modify, or incorporate the referenced implementation. License status and provenance are part of responsible curation.

## 11. Reproducibility Beats Authority

A result should be documented so another investigator can understand what was examined, how the result was obtained, and what limitations remain.

## 12. Classification Must Be Revisable

Taxonomies and clustering decisions are working instruments, not sacred truths. When evidence demonstrates that a classification is wrong, revise the classification and preserve the historical reasoning where useful.

## 13. No Silent Scope Expansion

Tiger Den's mission is mapping existing computational primitives. Adjacent ambitions—automatic synthesis, replacement libraries, package management, universal code archives, or other major functions—must not silently become project requirements.

## 14. Human Authority

The project owner remains the final authority over strategy, acceptance of consequential changes, and expansion of the operating model. Delegated technical stewardship does not remove that authority.

## 15. Stop When the Evidence Stops

If the next step requires a consequential assumption, unavailable evidence, outside authority, destructive action, or a change to project scope, stop and surface the decision rather than improvising.
